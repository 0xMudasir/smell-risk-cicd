## Per-class separation and the "3x rule"

Every consensus metric separates the classes with a **medium-to-large** effect
(two-sided Mann-Whitney U, p < 1e-300 full data; all survive Holm correction):

| Metric | clean median | smelly median | ratio | rank-biserial | band |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| Logical Lines | 7 | 56 | 8.0x | 0.607 | large |
| Total Operands | 33 | 252 | 7.6x | 0.583 | large |
| Volume / Bugs | 172 | 1930 | 11.3x | 0.577 | large |
| Length | 40 | 314 | 7.9x | 0.582 | large |
| Calculated Length | 75.6 | 411 | 5.4x | 0.541 | large |
| Vocabulary | 20 | 71 | 3.6x | 0.540 | large |
| Cyclomatic Complexity | 2 | 12 | 6.0x | 0.494 | medium |

**The paper's "3x rule" pattern appears - and is usually stronger.** Smelly
entities sit at 3.6x-11x the clean median. The paper reported roughly 3x for
security bugs; code smells amplify size/complexity metrics even more. Vocabulary
is the closest to a clean 3x. (Balanced-undersample Mann-Whitney in
`rq2_tests.json` agrees with the full-data result.)

## Thresholds and their validation (the fix the paper omits)

Thresholds use the paper's rule: **warning = smelly median, critical = smelly
Q3**. We then do what the paper never did - evaluate each threshold **as a
classifier** on the grouped-CV test folds (threshold fit on the train fold), and
add a **Youden-J** ROC threshold:

| Threshold type | typical F1 | behaviour |
|---|---|---|
| warning (smelly median) | 0.31 - 0.37 | moderate |
| critical (smelly Q3) | 0.32 - 0.36 | higher precision, lower recall |
| Youden-J (ROC) | 0.25 - 0.30 | high recall (0.63 - 0.89), low precision (0.15 - 0.20) |

**Finding: single-metric thresholds are descriptive, not deployable detectors.**
Best single-metric F1 is ~0.37 (Logical Lines warning). Youden maximises TPR-FPR
and therefore chases recall, but at a 9% base rate that floods false positives,
so precision collapses and F1 is worse than the median/Q3 rules. This is why RQ3
uses multivariate models, and why the paper's unvalidated thresholds should be
read as "where the smelly mass sits", not "a rule that detects smells".
