## Comparison to the paper (patterns, not numbers)

- **Tree ensembles win, and XGBoost is #1 - same as the paper.** The top of our
  board (XGBoost, AdaBoost, Decision Tree) is tree-based, mirroring the paper's
  XGBoost-then-ensembles ordering.
- **Our absolute AUCs are lower** (0.835 vs 0.91). Expected: different target
  (code smells), different and far smaller feature set (14 Halstead/size metrics
  vs 25 Understand metrics), and realistic grouped-by-project CV.
- **Where we diverge honestly:** our linear models do much better than the
  paper's (Logistic Regression 0.79 vs 0.51; both SVMs ~0.80 vs 0.69-0.71). On
  this feature set the classes are more linearly separable than the paper's were.
- **Naive Bayes collapses** (MCC -0.042, recall 0.99): it predicts almost
  everything smelly - the same degenerate behaviour the paper reported.
- F1 and MCC are modest across the board (best F1 ~0.39) because the task is hard
  at a 9% base rate with size-correlated features; this is why single-metric
  thresholds (RQ2) are weak and multivariate models are needed.

## Improvement track ("Ours")

- **Nested-CV tuning** (RandomizedSearchCV inside each train fold) lifts XGBoost
  to AUC 0.847 / PR 0.435 / F1 0.397 and Random Forest to 0.843 - the weak Table
  4 RF (10 trees, depth 3, AUC 0.794) gains most, concretely showing
  PAPER_REVIEW item 7.
- **SMOTE vs scale_pos_weight (XGBoost):** scale_pos_weight is marginally better
  (AUC 0.847 vs 0.835, F1 0.40 vs 0.393, recall 0.676 vs 0.641) and avoids
  synthesising rows - a reasonable default.
- **Decision-threshold tuning** (pooled out-of-fold XGBoost probabilities):
  default 0.5 gives F1 0.393; the max-F1 threshold (0.73) gives F1 0.422; a
  recall >= 0.80 operating point (threshold 0.25) gives recall 0.80 at precision
  0.21. The UI can expose this trade-off.
- **Size ablation (circularity proxy):** dropping the 5 size-dominant strict-
  consensus metrics lowers XGBoost AUC only 0.835 -> 0.816. The model keeps most
  of its signal without raw size, so the labels are **not** a trivial size
  threshold - consistent with the "plausible but unconfirmed" circularity verdict.
